<h3>Week 10</h3>
Minor scales
- Relative keys:
	- Major and minor key that share the same key signature. Each major has exactly one relative minor and each minor has exactly one relative major.
- Finding relative minor of given major key: 
	- Move down three half steps from the major tonic.
	- The relative minor of D major is B minor
- Parallel Keys:
	- Major key and minor key that share the same tonic. So... C major and c minor. Not ever key has a parallel
- Types of minor:
	- Natural minor:
		- The basic version from the key signature
	- Harmonic minor:
		- Raise the 7th scale degree to create a leading tone
	- Melodic minor: 
		- Raise the 6th and 7th scale degree on the way up, and then lower them on the way down (the way down is just like natural minor).

Harmonic and Melodic scales: 
- When altering notes for the scales, you do not change the key signature -> change accidentals instead.

Pentatonic: ( Smaller version of minor scales )
- 5 Notes -> Leave out the 4th and 7th scale degrees.

	- Simple intervals:

		- Intervals larger than an octave

- Parts of intervals
	- Quantity:
		- Count from the bottom note to the top note, inclusively. the bottom note is $1$, the top note is $n$, and the quantity of the interval is $n+1$
		- When counting to a sharp, there are no decimals:
			- G to F# is quantity $7$

		- Types of quantities:
			- Unison (U): quantity of $1$ (two of the same note)

	- Quality:
		- Five qualities: (Can only be one at a time)
			- Major:
				- Color intervals cam be minor, structure intervals cannot
			- Minor:
				- Color intervals can be minor, structure intervals cannot
				- The major interval's distance, but a half-step smaller
			- Perfect:
				- Structural intervals can be perfect, color intervals cannot
			- Augmented: (Aug / A)
				- Structured interval that is major/perfect(?) -> make it half step longer -> augmented
			- Diminished: (dim)
				- Structural interval that is minor -> make it a half step shorter -> diminished

<h1>Week 12</h1>
- Chords:
	- Composed of multiple notes
	- Vertical -> heard at the same time
	- Types of chords:
		- Triads:
			- 3 Notes that are thirds apart. (3 Notes having an interval quantity of 3 apart)
			- The interval between the bottom and top notes is P5
			- Triad components:
				1) Root: bottom note of the triad
				2) Third: note on top of the root, third above the root.
				3) Fifth: note on top of the third, fifth above the root.
			- The root of a triad names the triad. If the root of the triad is middle-C, then the triad is named "C triad"
			- Triads can have colors:
				- Major triad: 
					- Bottom: Major 3rd from third (middle note)
					- Top: Minor 3rd from third (middle note)
				- Minor triad:
					- Bottom Minor 3rd from third (middle note)
					- Top: Major 3rd from third (middle note) 
				- Diminished triad (Denoted with '$^o$'):
					- Bottom: Minor 3rd from third (middle note)
					- Top: Minor 3rd from third (middle note)
				- Augmented triad (Denoted with '+'):
					- Bottom: Major 3rd from third (middle note)
					- Top: Major 3rd from third (middle note)
		- Blocked Chord: 
			- All notes of the chord occur at the same time
		- Broken Chord:
			- Same notes of a broken chord, played at different times.
		- Implied Chord:
			- Part of an original chord is played. The listeners ears are filled in with some psychology bullshit.\

<h1>Roman Numerals</h1>

	II : Supertonic
	III : Mediant
