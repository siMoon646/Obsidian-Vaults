# Step 2: Analyze Your Game:
**Positive Feedback Loop:**
- Players are able to automate production of resources in the game. Automating the production of resources enables the player to use these automated resources to progress the game toward the objective more easily. Factories can be used to improve themselves through the resources that they make. 

**Negative Feedback Loop:**
- If a player wants to expand their factory, they will have to manage the power requirements of their factory along with the pollution that the expansion would entail. This negative feedback loop encourages the player factory to stay a size and complexity that meets their needs to an extent.

# Step 3: Reflection:
1) Feedback and Balance:
	- I think these feedback loops mostly promotes balanced gameplay. It encourages the player to prioritize planning of their factory, rather than trying to blast through the game with sheer scale of production.
	  
	  The player could place down thousands of machines to produce a single important resource at a high rate, but the negative feedback loop prevents them from doing that by making that single important resource consume a lot of power or by making the side effect of the relevant factory to produce that resource, make a lot of pollution.

2) Player Experience:
	- The positive feedback loop creates momentum in the player experience. Each successful factory provides more convenience to the player and the overall productivity of their factory. For example, one of the largest productivity boosts in the game comes when the player creates their first construction robots in the mid-game. This is because construction robots can help build more of the factory. This causes the factory to grow faster.

3) Design Adjustment:
	- I think that the feedback loops in the game are good, it becomes difficult to imagine how I might change them for the better. I have already spent ten minutes thinking about this and give up--everything I pick to change also has some poor trade-off in player experience or game pacing/balancing.

4) Design Trade-Off:
	- Well I was thinking that construction robots could be made available in the early-game in a cruder-form, but this would compromise the game's pacing. The early game doesn't last long and the factory in the early-game is usually not complex enough to justify construction robots. Introducing construction robots early-game would rush through it.
